# Product

## Register

product

## Users

A solo web-agency operator running Dispatch OS as their personal conversion CRM — the only user. They're heads-down between client work, checking the pipeline and the AI assistant dozens of times a day, not touring a dashboard. Every screen serves an active task: check a client's phase, draft a reply, log a note, approve an AI-proposed change.

## Product Purpose

A conversion-focused CRM + AI copilot for a one-person web agency's free-website-to-retainer pipeline. Tracks clients through a 10-phase intake playbook, generates outreach posts, and runs a guardrailed AI assistant that can read the CRM and propose writes (client updates, todos, timeline events, new client records) for the operator to approve or auto-apply. Success = the operator trusts it enough to run their whole pipeline through it without falling back to spreadsheets or memory.

## Brand Personality

Fast, sharp, no-nonsense. Built for someone who lives in this tool all day — density and speed beat decoration. No hand-holding copy, no marketing tone, no celebratory micro-interactions for routine actions.

## Anti-references

Generic SaaS admin-dashboard look: no purple/blue gradients, no cookie-cutter stat-card grids, no bloated sidebar with icon+label+badge on every item, no "welcome back" empty-state illustrations. This is an operator's cockpit, not a product demo.

## Design Principles

- Density over whitespace-for-its-own-sake — this is a tool used dozens of times a day, not browsed.
- Every panel/surface earns its screen space through an active task; nothing decorative.
- New surfaces (like the assistant panel) integrate into the existing app shell's own visual language — sidebar rail, card/line tokens, mono accents — rather than introducing a new sub-style.
- AI-proposed actions must always look distinct from confirmed CRM state — pending vs. applied is a first-class visual state, not an afterthought.
- Guardrails are visible, not hidden: confirm/auto mode and pending-action cards are core UI, not tucked away.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA — contrast, keyboard navigation, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected. No additional known requirements (single named user).
