# New Prospect Intake Playbook — Free Website Offer
 
What to text, in order, from the moment a prospect says yes through the retainer offer. Built from what actually worked with Tyler (HD Junk Removal) and what's playing out now with Zenith Junk Removal.
 
**Golden rules — apply at every phase:**
- One or two asks per message, max. Never an interrogation.
- Lowercase, casual, mirror his words back ("davis county" → "davis county plus 30 mile radius").
- Never mention hosting costs, pricing, or the retainer until Phase 9.
- Check what's already visible (FB page, prior messages) before asking for it again.
- If he goes quiet after a message, don't chase. One light follow-up, then let it sit.
---
 
### Phase 1 — Confirm & Kick Off
**Trigger:** He says yes / expresses interest.
**Goal:** Confirm enthusiasm, set the expectation you're starting, get the two easiest facts.
**Ask:** Business name + service area.
**Sample:** *"hey man awesome, glad youre down. ill get started this week. whats the business called and what areas do you service?"*
**Don't:** Mention pricing, hosting, or anything beyond the free build.
 
### Phase 2 — Services + Contact Info
**Goal:** Nail down what he actually does (drives site copy) and confirm the number to display.
**Skip:** If you're messaging via his FB business page, phone/email are usually already visible — don't ask for what you can already see.
**Sample:** *"what all do you handle - just junk removal or demo/appliance/debris too? and whats the best number for the site?"*
 
### Phase 3 — Photos & Socials
**Goal:** Get visual material, or confirm you'll fill gaps with stock.
**First:** Check his FB page yourself for usable job/truck photos before asking him for anything.
**If thin:** Ask, low-pressure.
**Sample:** *"if you got pics of jobs or the truck send a few over when you can, itll make the site look more like you. no worries if not, i can fill gaps for now."*
**Don't:** Let this block starting the build — newer businesses often have few or no photos, and that's fine.
 
### Phase 4 — Site Preferences
**Goal:** Pricing display + any must-have features.
**Ask:** Show numbers, or keep it "free estimates" only (most guys in this niche prefer the latter)? Anything specific he wants on the site?
**Sample:** *"want pricing shown on the site or keep it free-estimates only like most guys do? and is there anything specific youd want on there?"*
**Timing:** Once basics + photos are wrapped, not before.
 
### Phase 5 — Domain Check
**Goal:** Find out if he already owns a domain. This never blocks the build — a free subdomain works fine to start.
**Sample:** *"you got a domain already or nah? no rush either way, can get you set up on a free link for now and sort that out whenever."*
**Don't:** Push him toward buying one, or hint at future domain-handling pricing yet.
 
### Phase 6 — Mid-Build Check-In
**Trigger:** Site is roughly 50–70% done.
**Goal:** Keep him engaged, build anticipation, no ask attached.
**Sample:** *"quick peek at where its at - still filling in a few things but wanted you to see progress."* (attach a short video or screenshot)
**Don't:** Ask for anything in this message. It's purely a progress share.
 
### Phase 7 — Delivery
**Goal:** Hand it over plain, no strings.
**Sample:** *"as promised, heres your site. its yours, no catch."*
**Don't:** Mention pricing, upsells, or the retainer here. Let the delivery land on its own.
 
### Phase 8 — Zero-Ask Follow-Up
**Trigger:** A few days to a week after delivery.
**Goal:** Check how it's landing. This is the step that actually builds the trust — skipping it turns this into a one-time favor instead of a relationship.
**Sample:** *"hows the site treating you so far, any calls come through yet?"*
**Don't:** Attach any ask. If no calls yet, that's expected on a subdomain — not a bad sign, don't over-explain it unprompted.
 
### Phase 9 — Retainer Offer
**Trigger:** Zero-ask check-in has landed and felt natural (not necessarily the same message).
**Goal:** One low-pressure pitch, stated plainly.
**Terms:** $39/mo (he manages his own domain) or $49/mo (you handle the domain entirely) — no contract, cancel anytime.
**Sample:** *"hey, wanted to run something by you - i can keep the site hosted and maintained for $39/month if you handle the domain, or $49 if you want me to handle that too. no contract, cancel whenever."*
**If he defers** ("let me get a few more jobs first"): that's a soft yes-later, not a decline. Acknowledge low-pressure, don't re-pitch. Let him bring it back up, or reintroduce only on a real signal (e.g. he mentions being busier).
 
### Phase 10 — Growth System Upgrade
**Trigger:** Client has been on the $39/$49 retainer for 2+ months (default), or as early as 1 month in if he shows genuine interest on his own — asks about reviews, mentions missing calls, brings up wanting more presence. Don't force it early without a real signal.
**Goal:** One offer moment — not three separate upsells. He picks a tier (or stays on hosting as-is). Full tier breakdown, value framing, and guarantee live in `growth-system-upgrade-offer.md`.
**Note:** When he upgrades into any tier, the standalone hosting fee stops being billed — it's folded into the new price.
**Sample:** *"hey, wanted to run something by you since things have been rolling for a bit now. i put together a bigger system that folds in everything - hosting, your business listing, review requests, missed-call text-back, all in one dashboard instead of piecing it together. runs $197 to $799 a month depending on how much you want handled, no contract either way. want me to break down what's in each?"*
**Guarantee if asked:** no contract, cancel anytime, 14-day adjustment window if something's not set up right, no hidden fees. Never promise leads, calls, rankings, or local recognition — that's outcome territory, not something the service can honestly guarantee.
**Don't:** Pitch this to free-build or brand-new retainer clients — they go through the normal sequence first.
