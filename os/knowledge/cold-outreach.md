# Cold Outreach — first-touch DM flow

Canonical. Source: `_source/dm-copilot/SKILL.md` (the original call-booking
version), adapted to this agency's model. Covers a cold Facebook DM to a page
owner who has **not** yet agreed to anything — from the first hello to a yes on
the free build. Once they say yes, the conversation hands off to
`intake-playbook.md` at Phase 1.

Bound by `hard-rules.md` throughout. Cold outreach is constrained by account
health — keep volume slow (`acquisition-strategy.md`, Core Four).

## Terminal state

The original skill drove toward a **booked discovery call**. **This agency has
no call step** — confirmed 2026-09-04. Every channel is Facebook DMs and posts
only. The terminal state of a cold thread is **a yes to the free build**, which
then enters `intake-playbook.md` Phase 1. There is no discovery call, no
closer deck, no phone step anywhere in the model.

## The one rule that matters most

The reply has to sound like a human texting another human. Not a marketer, not
a salesperson, not a chatbot. Owners smell a sales DM instantly and ghost.

- Sounds the way a normal person texts — relaxed, a little informal.
- Short. One to three sentences. No paragraphs in a DM.
- No "I hope this finds you well", no "I wanted to reach out regarding".
- No em dashes. No exclamation spam, no emoji spam, no hype words.
- Never pitch a product mechanism in the DM. Never add "I'm not trying to sell
  you anything" — that disclaimer is itself the alarm.
- Match the prospect's energy. Short if they're short.

## The 4-stage flow (the map — hold it loosely)

**Stage 1 · Light open.** One sentence, zero business content. A human hello
that acknowledges the new connection. Goal: get a reply back.
*"hey man, thanks for the add. hows business going?"*

**Stage 2 · Numbers.** Once they engage, ask peer-level operator questions that
surface real detail — how they get work now, how much, how they handle quotes,
whether they have a website. One or two per message, never an interrogation.

**Stage 3 · Diagnosis.** Stop asking, start being useful. Reflect back one
piece of clarity they didn't have when the conversation started — owner to
owner. This is the stage most people skip, and skipping it is why cold threads
die. Not a pitch. A real read.

**Stage 4 · Offer the free build.** One low-pressure offer framed around what
you noticed: no website, or a weak one. State it plainly — a free site, they
own it, no strings, feedback is the only ask. Genuine operational cap if you
mention one. A yes moves to `intake-playbook.md` Phase 1.

## Handling curveballs (without breaking the no-pitch rule)

- **"so what do you actually do?"** — one plain line about the outcome of the
  deliverable, then a question back. No mechanism dump.
- **"what's it cost?"** — the build is at no cost; feedback is the only ask.
  Don't foreshadow the retainer.
- **"is this an AI thing / are you selling something?"** — acknowledge, stay
  casual, stay curious about their business. Lower the temperature.
- **cold / one-word replies** — one more light, genuine question. Then a soft
  exit that leaves the door open. Don't chase.

## Jargon translation

When the prospect uses a term, acronym, or piece of niche jargon the operator
might not know, decode it in one plain line so they never have to break the
conversation to look it up. One line, plain English, then back to the thread.
Skip if the operator clearly already knows it.

## Hot-path checklist (mirrored by `dm-copilot` skill, cold-outreach mode)

- [ ] Reply sounds like a real person texting — short, lowercase, no em dashes,
      no corporate opener, mirrors their words.
- [ ] One new ask or idea only.
- [ ] Correct stage identified (1–4), and the reply holds or advances it.
- [ ] Not skipping Stage 3 (diagnosis) to jump straight to the offer.
- [ ] No outcome promise. No mechanism pitch. No retainer/pricing foreshadowing.
- [ ] Jargon decoded if present.
- [ ] On a yes → hand to `intake-playbook.md` Phase 1.
- [ ] Volume stays slow (account health).
